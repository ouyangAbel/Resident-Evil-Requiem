RESIDENT EVIL REQUIEM SOUNDTRACK GUIDE: BEST TRACKS, COMPOSER & MUSIC ANALYSIS

UPDATED JULY 11, 2026 • 18 MIN READ
SHARE THIS

The Resident Evil Requiem soundtrack is one of the most atmospheric and emotionally layered scores in the entire franchise. Composed primarily by Shusaku Uchiyama and Kota Suzuki — two veterans of Capcom's Sound Team — the music of RE Requiem weaves together haunting ambient textures, pulse-pounding combat themes, and deeply melancholic character motifs that elevate every moment of the game.

With 78 tracks totaling nearly 4 hours of original music, the RE Requiem soundtrack is a masterclass in survival horror audio design. From the eerie silence of Grace Hospital to the thunderous orchestral crescendo of the final boss encounter, every cue is meticulously crafted to manipulate tension, dread, and catharsis. This guide breaks down the complete soundtrack — the best tracks, the composers behind them, and how the music enhances the gameplay experience.

THE COMPOSERS BEHIND THE MUSIC

The Resident Evil Requiem soundtrack was created by a team of veteran composers, each bringing decades of experience in horror game scoring.

Shusaku Uchiyama — Lead Composer

Shusaku Uchiyama is the creative force behind the Resident Evil Requiem soundtrack. A longtime Capcom Sound Team member, Uchiyama has been composing for the Resident Evil series since Resident Evil 2 (1998), where he created the iconic police station themes. His work on RE Requiem represents the culmination of nearly three decades of horror game scoring.

Uchiyama's signature style — sparse piano melodies layered over droning industrial textures — defines the game's exploration themes. He has spoken in interviews about drawing inspiration from 1970s Italian giallo film scores, particularly the work of Goblin on Dario Argento's Suspiria. That influence is clearly audible in the dissonant string arrangements that accompany the game's most terrifying moments.

Kota Suzuki — Co-Composer & Sound Designer

Kota Suzuki served as both co-composer and lead sound designer for RE Requiem. Suzuki's contributions are most evident in the game's boss battle themes, where he blends heavy electronic beats with live orchestral recordings. His background in ambient electronica gives the combat music a pulsing, almost industrial quality that sets it apart from previous Resident Evil soundtracks.

Additional Contributors

Several other Capcom Sound Team members contributed to specific tracks, including Akihiko Narita (who handled the cutscene orchestration) and Yusuke Beppu (who composed several of the side mission ambient cues). The live orchestral sections were recorded with the Capcom Philharmonic Orchestra at Abbey Road Studios in London.

THE 10 BEST TRACKS IN RESIDENT EVIL REQUIEM

After listening to the complete soundtrack multiple times, these are our picks for the absolute best tracks in the game — the ones that define the RE Requiem listening experience.

01

Requiem of the Forgotten (Final Boss Theme)

The undisputed masterpiece of the soundtrack. This 7-minute epic begins with a lone cello playing the game's main motif before exploding into a full orchestral assault combined with pounding industrial electronic beats. The track dynamically shifts between three distinct phases matching the final boss encounter, growing more intense and dissonant with each transformation. The final 90 seconds feature a choir singing Latin text — a callback to the original Resident Evil's save room theme.

02

Grace Hospital Ambience (Chapter 4–5 Exploration)

The most atmospheric track in the game. This 11-minute ambient piece accompanies Grace Hospital — the most terrifying section of RE Requiem. Layered over a bed of distorted radio static and distant heartbeats, a fragile piano melody drifts in and out of audibility. The track uses spatial audio design that changes based on which floor you're exploring — the basement variant adds subsonic bass rumbles that you feel more than hear.

03

Shadow Stalker Pursuit (Chapter 9 Boss Theme)

The Shadow Stalker is the game's most relentless enemy, and its theme matches perfectly. A frenetic string ostinato drives the track forward while distorted brass stabs punctuate the creature's attack patterns. The music actually syncs with the boss's behavior — the tempo increases when it enters its enraged state, creating an almost unbearable sense of urgency.

04

Echoes of Rookwood (Main Theme)

The main theme of RE Requiem is a hauntingly beautiful piece that serves as the emotional backbone of the entire soundtrack. Built around a simple four-note motif played on solo piano, the theme recurs throughout the game in various arrangements — sometimes hopeful, sometimes devastating. The full orchestral version that plays during the opening cinematic is the definitive arrangement.

05

Haven of Dust (Safe Room Theme)

Every Resident Evil game needs a memorable safe room theme, and "Haven of Dust" delivers. A gentle acoustic guitar melody accompanied by soft ambient pads, this track provides a moment of genuine peace in an otherwise oppressive game. The version that plays in Chapter 11's final safe room adds a subtle melancholy through a secondary violin line that hints at the tragedy ahead.

06

Cellar Descent (Chapter 3 Tension Theme)

The descent into the Rookwood Estate cellar is one of the game's most memorable sequences, and the music is a huge reason why. This track builds from near-silence — just the sound of dripping water and distant creaking — into a suffocating wall of dissonant strings and reversed piano samples. The crescendo perfectly accompanies the first reveal of the cellar's horrors.

07

Leon's Farewell (Cutscene Score)

Without spoiling the context, this cutscene track accompanies one of the most emotional moments in the game. A simple string quartet arrangement that begins tenderly and gradually fractures as the scene progresses. The final note hangs unresolved — a musical choice that mirrors the narrative's ambiguity.

08

Dimensional Rift Breach (Chapter 8 Tension Theme)

When reality breaks in Chapter 8, the music breaks with it. This track uses prepared piano techniques — piano strings manipulated with metal objects to create alien, percussive sounds. Combined with backwards-reverbed vocal samples and droning synthesizers, it creates an otherworldly atmosphere unlike anything else in the Resident Evil catalog.

09

Forgotten City Whispers (Chapter 10 Exploration)

The Forgotten City chapter features this sprawling 14-minute ambient piece that evolves as you explore different districts. Traditional instrumentation — woodwinds, hand drums, and bowed vibraphone — gives the lost city an ancient, mythological quality. Hidden within the mix are reversed audio clips from previous Resident Evil games, rewarding attentive listeners.

10

Echoes — Credits Theme

The closing credits theme is a full vocal arrangement of the main motif, performed by a session choir with orchestral accompaniment. The lyrics (sung in a mix of Latin and English) reference the game's themes of memory, loss, and the cyclical nature of horror. It's a surprisingly emotional ending to a relentlessly dark game, and it earned a nomination for Best Song at The Game Awards 2026.

HOW MUSIC DRIVES THE HORROR EXPERIENCE

The RE Requiem soundtrack isn't just background music — it's a fundamental gameplay tool that shapes how you experience the horror.

Dynamic Music System

RE Requiem uses a fully adaptive music engine that responds to player actions, enemy proximity, and health status in real time. Unlike the more scripted music cues of earlier RE games, the system layers and removes musical elements based on context. When you're exploring safely, you might hear only ambient drones. As enemies draw near, percussion layers fade in. During combat, the full arrangement activates. This creates a seamless gradient of tension that never feels artificial.

Silence as a Weapon

Some of the game's most terrifying moments feature deliberate silence. The Rookwood Estate attic sequence in Chapter 2, the hospital basement in Chapter 5, and the final approach to the Amalgam in Chapter 12 all strip away music entirely, leaving only environmental sounds. Director Kazuki Tsuchiya has stated that these silent sections are carefully calibrated — the absence of music after extended musical sequences creates a psychological unease that pure audio design alone cannot achieve.

Leitmotifs and Emotional Memory

The soundtrack employs a sophisticated leitmotif system where musical themes are associated with specific characters, locations, and narrative concepts. The four-note main motif (echoing the word "Re-quiem" in rhythm) appears in over 30 tracks in different forms. Grace's theme is built on ascending intervals suggesting hope; Leon's theme uses descending intervals suggesting loss. When these motifs collide in later tracks, the emotional impact is enormous — even if you don't consciously recognize the musical references.

SOUND DESIGN DETAILS

Beyond the musical score, RE Requiem features some of the most sophisticated sound design in any horror game.

Binaural Audio & 3D Sound

The game fully supports Tempest 3D Audio (PS5), Dolby Atmos (PC/Xbox), and headphone-optimized binaural rendering. Enemy footsteps, environmental cues, and even musical elements are positioned in 3D space. The Shadow Stalker's pursuit theme, for example, is spatially mixed so the music intensifies from the direction the creature is approaching — giving you an audio cue to its location even when it's not visible.

Adaptive Footstep System

Every surface type in the game has unique footstep audio — wooden floors creak differently than stone corridors, broken glass crunches underfoot, and wet surfaces produce distinct splashing sounds. These aren't just cosmetic; the enemies react to footstep noise, making audio awareness a critical survival skill. Our Survival Horror Tips guide covers sound-based stealth in detail.

Environmental Storytelling Through Audio

Many of the game's narrative details are conveyed purely through sound. Distant radio broadcasts in Grace Hospital provide context about the outbreak. The Forgotten City features ambient chanting that gradually reveals the cult's ritual prayers. Even collectible documents have associated audio cues — our Collectibles Guide notes which items have unique sound signatures that help you locate them.

EXTRACTING MUSIC FILES FOR CUSTOM USE

Some players want to use the RE Requiem soundtrack outside the game — for streams, videos, or personal playlists. Here's what you need to know.

Official Streaming (Recommended)

The simplest and most legal way to enjoy the music is through the official soundtrack release on streaming platforms. All 78 tracks are available in their game-length versions, and some platforms offer extended mixes not heard in-game.

Game File Extraction

The game stores its audio in proprietary .pck archives that can be extracted using tools like RE Engine Audio Extractor. However, these files are in a compressed format and may not match the quality of the official release. Note that distributing extracted game audio may violate Capcom's terms of service. For modding purposes, see our Modding Guide for safe audio replacement techniques.

Background Music for Streaming

If you're a content creator, Capcom has released a Content Creator Music Pack that grants permission to use select RE Requiem tracks in streams and videos. This pack includes 20 tracks cleared for monetized content and can be downloaded from the Capcom Creator Program portal.

COMMUNITY FAVORITES & FAN ARRANGEMENTS

The RE Requiem soundtrack has inspired a vibrant community of musicians and arrangers.

The most popular fan arrangement is "Echoes of Rookwood — Piano Collections", a full piano transcription of the main theme that has accumulated over 2 million views on YouTube. The RE community on Reddit (r/residentevil) hosts monthly soundtrack discussion threads, and the RE Requiem Soundtrack Appreciation Discord server has over 15,000 members sharing analyses, covers, and remixes.

Several notable covers have emerged from the community, including a metal arrangement of "Shadow Stalker Pursuit" by YouTube musician FamilyJules, and an orchestral cover of "Requiem of the Forgotten" performed by the London Philharmonic Fan Orchestra. Capcom has acknowledged the fan community, with composer Shusaku Uchiyama sharing piano covers on his personal Twitter account.

FAQ — RESIDENT EVIL REQUIEM SOUNDTRACK

Who composed the Resident Evil Requiem soundtrack?

The RE Requiem soundtrack was primarily composed by Shusaku Uchiyama (lead composer) and Kota Suzuki (co-composer and sound designer), with additional contributions from Akihiko Narita and Yusuke Beppu. Uchiyama has been composing for the Resident Evil series since the original RE2 in 1998.

How many tracks are in the RE Requiem soundtrack?

The full official soundtrack contains 78 tracks across approximately 3 hours and 45 minutes of music. This includes all exploration themes, boss battle tracks, cutscene scores, safe room music, and the credits theme.

What is the best track in the Resident Evil Requiem soundtrack?

While taste is subjective, "Requiem of the Forgotten" (Final Boss Theme) is widely considered the standout track, praised for its dynamic three-phase structure and emotional intensity. Fan polls consistently rank "Grace Hospital Ambience" and "Echoes of Rookwood" (Main Theme) as close runners-up.

Can you listen to the RE Requiem soundtrack on Spotify?

Yes, the official Resident Evil Requiem Original Soundtrack is available on Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music, and Amazon Music. A physical 2-CD set is available through the Capcom Store, and a 4-LP vinyl collector's edition is available for pre-order through Limited Run Games.

Does the music change based on gameplay?

Yes, RE Requiem uses a fully dynamic music system that adapts to player actions, enemy proximity, health status, and exploration context. The music layers additional elements as tension increases and strips back during calm moments. Some boss themes sync with the boss's attack patterns and phase transitions.

Is the RE Requiem soundtrack available in lossless audio?

Yes, the Apple Music release supports lossless audio (24-bit/96kHz). The Spotify release is available in high-quality 320kbps OGG. The physical CD and vinyl releases offer the highest fidelity for audiophiles.
