RE REQUIEM VS PREVIOUS GAMES: HOW IT COMPARES TO RE2R, RE4R, RE7 & VILLAGE

UPDATED JULY 8, 2026 • 18 MIN READ
SHARE THIS

Why This Comparison Matters: Resident Evil Requiem is the culmination of Capcom's modern survival-horror evolution. By comparing it side-by-side with RE7, RE2 Remake, Village, and RE4 Remake, we can see exactly how the franchise has transformed — and where Requiem pushes the series into new territory. Whether you're a longtime fan deciding if Requiem is worth your time, or a newcomer wondering which game to start with, this deep-dive comparison breaks down every major system.

The modern Resident Evil era — often called the "RE Engine era" — began in 2017 with Resident Evil 7: Biohazard and has produced some of the most critically acclaimed horror games ever made. With Resident Evil Requiem, Capcom has delivered what many consider the most ambitious entry yet, blending lessons learned from every preceding title into a single, massive experience.

But how does Requiem actually stack up against its predecessors? In this comprehensive comparison, we examine combat, horror design, storytelling, exploration, progression systems, and technical performance across all five modern RE titles to give you the definitive answer.

COMBAT SYSTEM EVOLUTION: FROM DESPERATION TO MASTERY

The combat system is where Requiem's evolution is most visible. Each modern RE game took a different approach to how the player fights, and Requiem synthesizes all of them.

RE7: The Desperate Beginning

RE7 stripped combat to its bare essentials. Ethan Winters was an ordinary man with no training — and the game made you feel it. Aiming was imprecise, movement was slow, and ammunition was genuinely scarce. The first-person perspective amplified the claustrophobia, and the Baker family encounters were as much about hiding as fighting. Combat was a last resort, not a first option.

RE2 Remake: Strategic Resource Play

RE2 Remake refined this into a more tactical system. The over-the-shoulder camera gave better spatial awareness, but Mr. X's persistent threat meant you could never feel safe. The "shoot the legs or the head?" decision became a genuine strategic choice, and the knife durability system added another layer of resource management. Combat was about making every bullet count.

Village: Action-Horror Balancing Act

Village swung the pendulum toward action. Ethan could now block attacks, the Duke's shop made upgrading weapons easier, and encounters grew more numerous. The Lycan hordes in the opening hours were a clear statement: this game wants you to fight. Village's combat felt like a middle ground between RE7's horror and RE4's action.

RE4 Remake: Precision Action

RE4 Remake perfected the action-horror combat loop. Leon's roundhouse kicks, the parry system with the knife, crowd-control crowd management, and the satisfying weapon upgrade economy made combat fun in a way no previous RE game had achieved. It was less scary but more mechanically deep.

Requiem: The Adaptive System

Requiem's combat is the series' most sophisticated. It introduces what Capcom calls the "Adaptive Threat System" — enemies dynamically change their behavior based on your playstyle. Play aggressively, and enemies become more evasive and aggressive. Play conservatively, and they become more bold, trying to corner you. The dual-protagonist system also means you switch between Leon's action-oriented toolkit and Jill's more methodical survival approach, sometimes within the same chapter.

Verdict: Requiem offers the deepest combat in the series. RE4 Remake has the most satisfying moment-to-moment action, while RE7 remains the scariest. Requiem threads the needle between all of them.

HORROR DESIGN: SCARES VS. TENSION

Each modern RE game approached horror differently, and understanding these philosophies reveals what makes Requiem unique.

Game Horror Type Signature Scare Fear Factor
RE7 Psychological, visceral Jack Baker stalking through walls ★★★★★
RE2R Tension, resource anxiety Mr. X footsteps (constant dread) ★★★★☆
Village Atmospheric, setpiece-driven House Beneviento (baby monster) ★★★☆☆
RE4R Action-horror, dread Regenerador breathing in the dark ★★★☆☆
Requiem Adaptive, escalating Nemesis-class pursuers + sanity system ★★★★☆

RE7 remains the purest horror experience — its Baker family encounters are still unmatched for raw terror. RE2 Remake's horror came from anticipation: Mr. X's boots on the floor above you created a constant low-level dread. Village's House Beneviento sequence was a standout, but the rest of the game leaned more toward action. RE4 Remake was primarily an action game with horror aesthetics.

Requiem introduces a "Sanity" mechanic that blurs the line between real threats and hallucinations. As your sanity drops, the environment distorts — shadows move, whispers become audible, and enemies may or may not be real. This creates a uniquely unsettling experience that none of the previous games attempted. Combined with its adaptive pursuer system (enemies that learn your routes), Requiem generates dread in a way that feels fresh without relying on jump scares.

STORYTELLING & NARRATIVE STRUCTURE

The storytelling approach has varied wildly across the modern RE era:

RE7 told an intimate, self-contained story through Ethan's eyes. The Bakers were compelling villains, and the first-person perspective made every plot beat feel personal. However, its connections to the broader RE lore were minimal until the final act.

RE2 Remake was a masterclass in environmental storytelling. The interwoven Leon A/Claire B scenarios, the tragic Sherry subplot, and the slow revelation of Umbrella's crimes through documents and emails created a rich narrative tapestry — all without lengthy cutscenes.

Village aimed for epic scope but stumbled with pacing. The four-lord structure felt episodic, and Ethan's emotional arc — while it paid off in the ending — was undercut by his flat characterization. Mother Miranda was an underused villain.

RE4 Remake improved the original's story dramatically. Leon's quips were better integrated, Ashley was a more capable companion, and Luis got the character development he deserved. It proved RE could have both great gameplay and a solid narrative.

Requiem's Approach: Requiem features the most ambitious story in the series. The dual-protagonist structure (Leon and Jill) allows Capcom to tell parallel narratives that converge in the third act. For the first time since RE5, longtime characters are given real emotional weight — Leon's PTSD from Raccoon City and Jill's trauma from the Nemesis incident are central themes, not afterthoughts. The story spans six global locations and ties together threads from RE7, Village, and the classic series in a way that feels earned rather than fan-servicey.

EXPLORATION & WORLD DESIGN

World design is where Requiem makes its boldest departure from its predecessors.

RE7 used a single-location design (the Baker estate) that created incredible density. Every room mattered, every drawer could hide a key item, and the interconnected layout rewarded careful exploration.

RE2 Remake expanded this to the Raccoon City Police Department — still a single hub, but larger and more labyrinthine. The RPD is one of gaming's greatest locations, and its master key-and-lock design remains a gold standard for level design.

Village tried a semi-open structure with the village as a central hub connecting distinct areas. It was more expansive but less dense — some areas felt sparse compared to RE7's compact estate.

RE4 Remake was the most linear of the bunch, with chapters flowing in a mostly fixed sequence. However, it compensated with incredible variety — each chapter felt like a different game, from the village siege to the castle to the island.

Requiem takes the boldest approach: a semi-open world divided into six major locations (a ruined city, an underground research facility, a tropical island, a European cathedral, an arctic base, and a final zone we won't spoil). Each location is the size of RE2R's RPD, with its own internal logic, key items, and secrets. You can tackle some locations in any order, and your choices affect which story scenes you encounter and which ending you unlock.

How you grow stronger has evolved significantly:

RE7 and RE2 Remake kept progression simple: find better guns, find upgrades for them. Village introduced the Duke and his shop, adding a light RPG layer. RE4 Remake expanded this with weapon tuning, treasure combining, and a more robust crafting system.

Requiem goes further with a dual-currency system (Lei for purchases, Synth Tokens for weapon modifications), character-specific skill trees for Leon and Jill, and multiple vendor NPCs across its six locations. GRAPHICS & TECHNICAL PERFORMANCE

The RE Engine has evolved with each release:

RE7 (RE Engine v1.0) was a revelation at launch — photorealistic interiors, incredible lighting, and smooth performance even on modest hardware. It proved Capcom could compete with the best in visual fidelity.

RE2 Remake refined the engine for third-person gameplay. The rain-soaked RPD, the detailed zombie dismemberment, and the flashlight system were all technically impressive. Performance was excellent across platforms.

Village pushed the engine into larger environments. The outdoor vistas, the castle interiors, and especially the lighting during Dimitrescu's castle sections were gorgeous. However, some indoor areas showed asset repetition.

RE4 Remake (RE Engine v2.0) was the technical high point until Requiem. It featured vastly improved character models, denser foliage, better particle effects, and a more sophisticated global illumination system. The PS5 version ran at a rock-solid 60fps with ray tracing enabled.

Requiem runs on RE Engine v3.0, which introduces several new technologies: real-time global illumination with ray-traced reflections, a new hair and fabric simulation system, volumetric fog that reacts to player movement, and significantly improved facial animation. On PS5 and Xbox Series X, it targets 60fps at dynamic 4K; on PC, it scales beautifully with DLSS 4 and FSR 3.1 support. For PC-specific settings recommendations, see our Best Settings Guide.

DIFFICULTY & REPLAYABILITY

Replayability varies significantly across the series:

RE7 had Madhouse difficulty and the Not a Hero DLC, but replay value was moderate. The game was designed to be experienced once for maximum impact.

RE2 Remake excelled here with its 2nd Run scenarios, multiple difficulty modes, and the inherent replayability of Leon A/Claire B combinations. The Tofu Survivor mode added humor and challenge.

Village offered the Mercenaries mode and multiple difficulties, but the linear structure limited how different a second playthrough could feel.

RE4 Remake was built for replayability: Professional difficulty, New Game+, the unlockable weapons, and the addictive Mercenaries mode kept players coming back for months.

Requiem is the most replayable entry in the series. Its dual-protagonist structure means Leon's playthrough and Jill's playthrough have distinct sections, encounters, and even story beats. The six-location structure allows different exploration orders, multiple endings (four confirmed so far), and a robust New Game+ system with carry-over upgrades. The unlockable difficulty modes include "Nightmare" (a permadeath variant) and "Classic" (fixed camera angles as an homage to the original trilogy). WHICH GAME SHOULD YOU PLAY FIRST?

If you're new to the series, here's our recommended play order:

Recommended Play Order for Newcomers

  1. RE2 Remake — The best entry point. Modern controls, great story, and it teaches survival-horror fundamentals.
  2. RE7 — Experience pure horror. Play in a dark room with headphones for maximum effect.
  3. Village — The direct sequel to RE7, with a broader scope and more accessible gameplay.
  4. RE4 Remake — The pinnacle of action-horror. Best played after you understand the series' mechanics.
  5. Requiem — The grand finale. It rewards series veterans with callbacks and lore connections, but is still enjoyable for newcomers.

FAQ: RE REQUIEM VS PREVIOUS GAMES

Is RE Requiem scarier than RE7?

It depends on what scares you. RE7 relies on jump scares, tight corridors, and the Baker family's unpredictability. Requiem uses a Sanity mechanic that creates psychological dread — hallucinations, distorted environments, and enemies that may or may not be real. Most players find RE7's opening hours scarier, but Requiem's mid-game is more consistently unsettling.

Does RE Requiem play like RE4 Remake?

The combat shares DNA — both use over-the-shoulder aiming and a focus on crowd management — but Requiem's Adaptive Threat System means enemies respond to your strategies in real time. It also includes a first-person toggle, so you can play it like RE7/Village if you prefer. The dual-protagonist system adds variety that RE4R doesn't have.

Do I need to play the other RE games to understand Requiem's story?

No, but it helps. Requiem's story is self-contained enough to enjoy on its own, but longtime fans will get much more out of Leon and Jill's character arcs. At minimum, playing RE2 Remake and RE7 will give you the context for the two most important story threads.

Is RE Requiem longer than RE4 Remake?

Yes. A single playthrough takes roughly 20-25 hours (compared to RE4R's 15 hours), and the dual-protagonist structure means a full completionist run can exceed 50 hours. It's the longest modern RE game by a significant margin.

Which RE game has the best graphics?

Requiem, by a wide margin. RE Engine v3.0 introduces ray-traced global illumination, advanced hair simulation, and volumetric effects that surpass even RE4 Remake. However, RE7's art direction still holds up remarkably well for a 2017 game.

Is RE Requiem worth buying if I already own RE4 Remake?

Absolutely. While RE4R has tighter, more focused action, Requiem offers a completely different experience with its dual protagonists, Sanity mechanic, semi-open world structure, and deeper RPG progression. They complement each other rather than compete.

FINAL VERDICT: WHERE REQUIEM RANKS

Every modern Resident Evil game is excellent — that's the remarkable thing about the RE Engine era. But each excels in different areas:

The Definitive Ranking

🥇 RE Requiem — The most complete package. Deepest combat, most content, best replayability, and the most ambitious story. Minor pacing issues in the second act prevent it from being perfect.

🥈 RE4 Remake — The tightest, most polished experience. Every system clicks perfectly. Less ambitious than Requiem but more consistently excellent.

🥉 RE2 Remake — The best pure survival horror. The RPD remains gaming's greatest level design. Limited only by its shorter length and simpler combat.

4th: RE7 — The scariest game in the series. Its first-person horror is unmatched. Combat and replayability are its weakest points.

5th: Village — The most accessible entry. Great for newcomers, but the uneven pacing and underused villains hold it back from the top tier.

Resident Evil Requiem is the culmination of everything Capcom has learned over the past decade. Whether it's your first RE game or your fifteenth, it's an essential experience — and one that rewards you more the deeper you go.
