Resident Evil Requiem features one of the most robust photo modes in the entire survival horror franchise. Whether you want to capture the haunting beauty of Rookwood Estate, freeze a terrifying Lycan mid-lunge, or discover hidden details only visible through the camera lens, this comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about RE Requiem's photo mode — from basic controls to advanced composition techniques.

How to Access Photo Mode in RE Requiem

Unlike many games that lock photo mode behind a menu, Resident Evil Requiem makes it instantly accessible during gameplay. The method varies slightly depending on your platform:

PlatformHow to Open Photo Mode
PCPress P key (default binding)
PlayStation 5Hold L3 + R3 simultaneously
Xbox Series X|SHold LS + RS simultaneously

Photo mode pauses the game entirely, giving you unlimited time to compose your shot. You can access it during cutscenes, exploration, combat — even during certain boss fight animations. However, some story-critical cutscenes disable photo mode temporarily, indicated by a small lock icon in the corner of the screen.

Pro Tip: If you're playing on PC, you can rebind the photo mode key in Settings > Controls > Camera > Photo Mode. Many players prefer binding it to a side mouse button for quick access during intense moments.

Complete Camera Controls Breakdown

RE Requiem's photo mode gives you full six-axis camera freedom. Here's what every control does and when to use it:

Camera Position (X, Y, Z Axis)

The left stick (or WASD on PC) moves the camera freely in three dimensions. You can fly the camera through walls within a limited radius around your character — roughly 15 meters in any direction. This restriction prevents you from seeing out-of-bounds areas but still allows dramatic repositioning.

Camera Rotation & Tilt

The right stick (or mouse) controls rotation. You can tilt the camera up to 90 degrees in any direction, enabling dutch angles, bird's-eye views, and ground-level perspectives. The tilt function is particularly useful for creating dramatic low-angle shots of towering enemies like the Forgotten King.

Field of View (FOV)

Adjustable from 15° to 120°. A narrow FOV (15-30°) creates a telephoto compression effect — perfect for portrait-style character shots. A wide FOV (90-120°) produces dramatic perspective distortion, ideal for showcasing the massive environments in Chapter 9's Forgotten City.

Focal Distance & Depth of Field

This is where RE Requiem's photo mode truly shines. You can set a focal point anywhere in the scene, then adjust the depth of field blur from f/1.4 (extreme bokeh) to f/22 (everything sharp). The blur quality is exceptional — Capcom used a physically-based bokeh simulation that produces smooth, circular highlights rather than the harsh polygonal blur seen in lesser implementations.

Hidden Details Only Visible in Photo Mode

One of the most exciting aspects of RE Requiem's photo mode is discovering details that are nearly impossible to notice during normal gameplay. Data miners and eagle-eyed players have uncovered several secrets:

The Rookwood Family Portrait Easter Egg

In Chapter 2, the large portrait hanging in the main hall of Rookwood Estate appears to be a standard painting during gameplay. However, zooming in with photo mode reveals that one of the family members has red eyes — a subtle hint about the family's connection to the Las Plagas parasite. This detail is only visible at maximum zoom with the aperture set to f/22 for maximum sharpness.

Shadow Stalker's Eyes in the Darkness

During the Shadow Stalker ambush sequence in Chapter 9, the creature hides in dark corners before attacking. If you pause and enter photo mode at the exact right moment, you can see its glowing red eyes watching you from the shadows — up to 30 seconds before it actually attacks. This confirms the creature's AI tracks you long before the scripted encounter.

Hidden Map Coordinates

Several documents and papers scattered throughout the game contain what appear to be random numbers. Photo mode reveals they are actually GPS coordinates pointing to real-world locations associated with the game's development — Capcom's Osaka headquarters, the Czech village that inspired the Rookwood Estate, and a cemetery in London referenced in the game's lore.

Best Locations for Stunning Screenshots

RE Requiem's environments are meticulously crafted, and certain locations produce particularly striking photographs. Here are the top spots:

1. Rookwood Estate Main Hall (Chapter 1-2)

The grand staircase with its wrought-iron railings and shattered stained-glass windows creates gorgeous light shafts. Position the camera at ground level looking up for a dramatic perspective. Use the Vintage Horror filter at 65% for an authentic gothic atmosphere.

2. Grace Hospital Rooftop (Chapter 5)

The rain-slicked rooftop offers panoramic views of the storm-battered cityscape. The lightning strikes are random, but if you're patient, you can capture a bolt illuminating the entire scene. Use Cold Steel filter with exposure at -1.0 to emphasize the lightning.

3. The Forgotten City (Chapter 9)

This massive underground metropolis features bioluminescent fungi that cast an otherworldly green-purple glow. The scale is breathtaking — set FOV to 100°+ and position the camera at the edge of the cliff for a vista shot. Neon Decay at 50% enhances the alien atmosphere.

4. The Dimensional Rift (Chapter 8)

The reality-warping effects in this chapter create unique visual distortions that look incredible in still images. The floating debris and cracked space-time textures produce surreal compositions. Use the default filter with high contrast (+40) and narrow FOV for an abstract feel.

5. The Final Descent (Chapter 11)

The descent into the abyss features cascading waterfalls of blood-red liquid and towering organic structures. Position the camera below the falls looking up for a vertigo-inducing shot. Abyssal filter with slight exposure boost (+0.5) works beautifully here.

Lighting Tip: RE Requiem's lighting engine produces the most dramatic shadows during what the community calls "golden moments" — brief 2-3 second windows where the light source shifts angle during certain animations. Learn these moments and you'll capture shots with Hollywood-quality lighting.

Advanced Composition Techniques

Rule of Thirds Grid

Press G (PC) or D-pad Down (consoles) to toggle a rule-of-thirds grid overlay. This helps you position key elements at intersection points for more visually pleasing compositions. The grid is invisible in the final screenshot.

Leading Lines

RE Requiem's environments are full of natural leading lines — corridors, fences, blood trails, shadows. Use these to guide the viewer's eye toward your focal point. The long hallways in Rookwood Estate are particularly effective for this technique.

Frame Within a Frame

Doorways, windows, and arches in the game create natural frames. Position your camera so a character or enemy is visible through one of these frames for a layered, cinematic composition. The archways in Chapter 10's Convergence area are perfect for this.

Silhouette Shots

Position a bright light source behind your subject (enemies work best) and lower the exposure to -2.0 or below. This creates dramatic silhouettes against the light. The Lycan enemies, with their distinctive hunched silhouettes, are ideal subjects.

Platform-Specific Tips

PC: NVIDIA Ansel Integration

On PC with NVIDIA GPUs, RE Requiem supports Ansel super-resolution capture. This allows you to take screenshots at up to 8K resolution regardless of your display, then downsample for crystal-clear 1080p or 4K output. Enable Ansel mode by pressing Alt+F2 while in photo mode.

Console: Screenshot Sharing

PS5 screenshots are saved as .jpg or .png (configurable in system settings). For the highest quality, set PS5 to save as PNG. Xbox captures at native resolution and can upload directly to OneDrive. Both platforms support HDR screenshots — RE Requiem's photo mode includes an HDR tone-mapping slider to fine-tune how the HDR image looks when viewed on non-HDR displays.

HDR Note: If you're sharing screenshots on social media (which strips HDR metadata), use the "SDR Preview" toggle in photo mode to see exactly how your shot will look when viewed on standard displays.

FAQ — RE Requiem Photo Mode

Can I use photo mode during boss fights?

Yes! Photo mode works during all boss encounters in RE Requiem. The game pauses completely when photo mode is active, so you can safely compose shots even during the most intense battles. This makes boss fights excellent opportunities for dramatic screenshots — the boss animations are often frozen mid-attack, creating dynamic action poses.

Do photo mode screenshots count toward achievements?

No, photo mode is purely cosmetic and has no effect on gameplay, achievements, or progression. You can use it as often as you like without any impact on your save file or trophy progress. For trophy guidance, check our Trophies & Achievements Guide.

Can I remove the HUD in photo mode?

The HUD is automatically hidden when you enter photo mode. However, if you want to remove the HUD during normal gameplay for cleaner screenshots without entering photo mode, go to Settings > Display > HUD and set it to "Minimal" or "Off." This is especially useful for capturing ambient shots where you don't want to pause the action.

Is there a way to share screenshots directly from the game?

RE Requiem doesn't have a built-in social sharing feature, but the game saves screenshots to a dedicated folder: Documents/My Games/RE Requiem/Screenshots/ on PC, or your system's capture gallery on console. From there, you can share to any platform. The game also supports PlayStation's built-in sharing and Xbox's share features natively.

What's the best resolution for screenshots?

Capture at the highest resolution your hardware supports. On PC, if you have an NVIDIA GPU, use Ansel's super-resolution to capture at 4K or 8K even on a 1080p display — the downscaled result will be noticeably sharper. On console, PS5 captures at native 4K in Quality Mode, while Performance Mode captures at 1440p upscaled to 4K.

Can I use ReShade with photo mode?

Yes, ReShade works alongside the built-in photo mode. Many virtual photographers use ReShade for additional effects like ambient occlusion enhancement, custom color grading, or cinematic letterboxing on top of the game's built-in filters. See our Modding Guide for ReShade installation instructions.

Final Tips for Aspiring Virtual Photographers

The best photo mode screenshots in RE Requiem come from patience and experimentation. Don't just snap the first frame — spend time adjusting the camera angle, trying different filters, and playing with the depth of field until the composition feels right. The game's environments are rich with detail, and there's always something new to discover when you slow down and look closely.

Remember that horror photography is about atmosphere, not clarity. Some of the most haunting screenshots use darkness, negative space, and ambiguity to create unease. Don't be afraid to let shadows dominate your frame — what you can't see is often more terrifying than what you can.
