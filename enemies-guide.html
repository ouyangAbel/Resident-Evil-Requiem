ENEMY OVERVIEW & DIFFICULTY SCALING

Resident Evil Requiem features 14 distinct enemy types spread across its three-act structure. Enemy design follows a clear escalation: Act I introduces basic infected and Lycans, Act II adds ranged and mechanical threats, and Act III unleashes supernatural enemies that break the rules of conventional combat.

Enemies scale significantly with difficulty. Below is how the core stats change:

PRO TIP

Know When to Fight and When to Run

Not every enemy needs to die. On Hardcore and Professional, ammo conservation is critical. Infected Staff and other slow enemies can often be dodged or stunned with a leg shot. Save your heavy ammo for Crimson Lycans, Shadow Wraiths, and boss encounters.

Former estate staff transformed by the T-Virus outbreak. These are your basic shambling enemies, present throughout Chapters I and II. Their movement is slow and predictable, making them ideal for practicing combat fundamentals.

Attack Pattern: Slow shamble followed by a telegraphed grab (1.2s windup). Occasionally lunges forward if within 3 meters.

Strategy: The most ammo-efficient approach is a single leg shot to stun, followed by a knife finisher. This saves handgun ammo for emergencies and is essential practice for higher difficulties. If overwhelmed, a single headshot staggers them long enough to run past.

Infected estate hounds that hunt in packs of two to three. Faster than standard infected but with lower HP. Their howl telegraphs an incoming pounce attack from roughly 8 meters.

Attack Pattern: Fast sprint into a leaping bite. Can dodge sideways to avoid. After a missed pounce, they have a 1.5s recovery window.

Strategy: The shotgun is the ideal weapon — one blast at close range kills outright. With a handgun, two well-placed headshots finish them. Listen for the distinct howl that signals their approach; it is your best early-warning system.

The Chapter II mini-boss. A fast, aggressive enemy wielding a bone saw. She is the first significant difficulty spike and teaches players the dodge-punish loop that defines RE Requiem combat.

Attack Pattern: Three attacks: (1) horizontal saw slash — fast, medium damage, dodge backward; (2) lunging stab — telegraphed by a 1-second scream, high damage, dodge sideways and punish with 2 shotgun blasts; (3) frenzy combo — 4 rapid slashes in a forward cone, triggered below 50% HP, keep distance.

Strategy: The fight takes place in a cluttered attic. Use furniture as obstacles to create space. Wait for the lunging stab telegraph — dodge sideways, fire two shotgun blasts to the head, retreat. Repeat 5-6 times on Standard. Do not engage during the frenzy combo.

Miners and smugglers infected in the tunnels beneath Rookwood Estate. Tougher than surface infected and often encountered in water-flooded chambers where movement is slowed.

Attack Pattern: Emerges from water or debris piles. Uses a slower but harder-hitting grab than Infected Staff. The grab deals roughly 1.5x the damage of a standard infected grab.

Strategy: Use raised platforms in flooded rooms for better positioning. The narrow tunnel corridors work in your favor — funnel Tunnelers into single-file lines for easy headshots. Two headshots stagger, three kill on Standard.

ACT II ENEMIES (CHAPTERS IV-VI)

CRIMSON LYCAN
FIRST APPEARANCE: CHAPTER IV — THE CRIMSON WING
THREAT LEVEL: ★★★★★
HP (Standard)
3,200
Headshot Stagger
3 shots
Best Weapon
Shotgun / Rifle
Weak Point
Head

The most dangerous common enemy in the game. Crimson Lycans are faster, tougher, and more aggressive than standard Lycans, with a red-tinted hide and glowing eyes. They hunt in coordinated packs and can leap across entire rooms.

Attack Pattern: (1) claw swipe combo — 3 rapid swipes in a forward arc, dodge backward; (2) leaping slam — jumps from up to 10 meters, lands with AoE damage; (3) howl — stuns Leon for 1 second if within 5 meters, followed by an immediate charge; (4) wall climb — scales walls and ceilings for ambush attacks.

Strategy: Do not fight Crimson Lycans at close range. Use the Semi-Auto Rifle to engage from distance. The shotgun is your emergency weapon — 3 blasts stagger, 4 kill on Standard. The Chapter IV main hall ambush sends three waves of 2-3 Crimson Lycans; use the balcony railings for height advantage and funnel them through doorways. On Professional, a single Crimson Lycan kills in two hits — prioritize them above all other enemies.

Tougher, armored humanoid enemies that appear after the mansion transforms. Infected Guards wear partial body armor that reduces handgun damage to the torso by roughly 30%.

Attack Pattern: Standard melee attacks, but with faster windup and less recovery time than basic infected. Some variants carry melee weapons (batons, fire axes) that deal significantly more damage.

Strategy: Aim for the head to bypass armor. Handgun takes 4 headshots to kill; shotgun takes 2 blasts. Leg shots are less effective due to armored greaves. If a Guard is carrying a weapon, prioritize them — one axe swing can take 40% of your HP on Standard.

Automated security drones that patrol the underground laboratory. Unlike organic enemies, they do not stagger from headshots and require precision targeting of their central lens.

Attack Pattern: Hovers at ceiling height (roughly 3-4 meters). Fires a targeting laser for 1 second before shooting a burst of 3 energy bolts. Bolts travel in a straight line and can be dodged with a well-timed sidestep.

Strategy: Shoot the glowing central lens. The Semi-Auto Rifle kills in 2-3 lens hits. Drones are often encountered in pairs — use narrow corridors to engage them one at a time. Their energy bolts deal moderate damage but have perfect accuracy; do not stand still while aiming.

Two enemy variants found in the laboratory. Infected Scientists are similar to Infected Staff but slightly faster. Test Subjects are the laboratory's failed experiments — larger, with glowing growths on their bodies that explode on death, dealing AoE damage.

Attack Pattern (Test Subjects): Slow, lumbering movement. Uses a heavy overhead slam with long reach. On death, their glowing growths detonate after a 2-second delay, dealing splash damage in a 3-meter radius.

Strategy: Kill Test Subjects from range. The death explosion can kill nearby Infected Scientists, making them useful for crowd control. If a Test Subject approaches, shoot its legs to slow it, then retreat before finishing it off. A grenade clears a group of Scientists efficiently.

ACT III ENEMIES (CHAPTERS VII-XII)

CULTISTS

THREAT LEVEL: ★★★☆☆
HP (Standard)
1,400
Headshot Stagger
2 shots
Best Weapon
Rifle / Shotgun
Weak Point
Head, unarmored torso

Robed cultists wielding ceremonial daggers. They are the primary humanoid enemies of Act III, encountered in the cathedral town and its surrounding areas. Faster than Infected Guards and often attack in coordinated groups.

Attack Pattern: Dagger slash combos (2-3 hits). Some Cultists throw daggers from range, dealing moderate damage. Their AI coordinates — one Cultist will flank while others attack from the front.

Strategy: Use the environment to break line of sight. Cultists are vulnerable to headshots but their hoods can absorb one hit. Rifle headshots through the hood still stagger on the second hit. Shotgun blasts at close range kill in 1-2 hits. Watch your flanks — always know where all Cultists are.

Spectral hounds that phase between the physical and ethereal planes. While ethereal, they take 50% reduced damage from conventional weapons. They appear in packs of 3-5 and are frequently mixed with other enemy types in arena encounters.

Attack Pattern: Phases into ethereal form and dashes through the player, dealing damage on contact. Materializes behind you for a follow-up bite. The dash leaves a visible blue trail.

Strategy: The Shotgun is the most reliable weapon — one blast forces a Phantom Hound out of ethereal form and staggers it. Wait until they materialize for a bite attempt, then fire. Do not waste rifle ammo on ethereal targets; the damage reduction makes it inefficient. In arena encounters, kill Phantom Hounds first — their flanking dashes are the most disruptive attack pattern in group fights.

Heavily armored knights that guard the cathedral and its inner chambers. Heavily resistant to frontal attacks, with a large crystalline growth on their back that serves as their weak point.

Attack Pattern: Slow but devastating overhead hammer slam (AoE, high damage). Frontal shield bash that staggers Leon. After attacking, they pause for 2 seconds before their next move.

Strategy: Never attack from the front. The shield blocks roughly 90% of frontal damage. Bait the overhead slam, dodge sideways, then fire at the exposed back crystal during the 2-second recovery window. The Broken Butterfly Magnum kills in two crystal hits. If you lack magnum ammo, grenades deal full damage through the shield. Two Guardians together is one of the hardest non-boss encounters — use the arena to separate them.

The signature enemy of RE Requiem's late game. Shadow Wraiths phase between visible and invisible states, appearing as humanoid silhouettes of swirling black smoke. They can only be damaged while visible and leave a faint shimmer effect when invisible.

Attack Pattern: Cycles between 6-8 seconds invisible (drifting toward the player) and 2-3 seconds visible (attacking). Invisible: moves slowly, leaves shimmer trail. Visible: fast claw swipes with high damage. Can grab from invisibility and drain health over 3 seconds.

Strategy: Watch for the shimmer effect — it is the only way to track them while invisible. Engage with the Semi-Auto Rifle at maximum range while they are visible; retreat while they are invisible. Shotgun blasts at close range stagger them out of invisibility, creating a damage window. Do not let them get behind you. Two to three rifle headshots kill on Standard, but their phasing makes landing consecutive shots challenging.

The final common enemy type, encountered in the convergence arenas of Chapter X. Enraged Sentinels are the game's tank enemies — massive armored figures that serve as damage sponges and area denial in arena encounters.

Attack Pattern: Overhead slam (AoE), charging bull rush (tracks player, destroys cover), ground pound (stuns everything in 5-meter radius). Very slow telegraph on all attacks. Cannot be staggered by conventional weapons.

Strategy: Enraged Sentinels are designed to force you out of position while other enemies attack. Kill all smaller enemies first, then focus the Sentinel. The Broken Butterfly Magnum is the only weapon that deals efficient damage — 3 headshots kill on Standard. Grenades deal good damage but require precise timing to hit during the long telegraph animations.

COMBAT TIPS & AMMO ECONOMY

TIP 1

Leg Shot + Knife Finish Is Your Best Friend

Against Infected Staff, Infected Tunnelers, and Scientists, one leg shot stuns them for a knife finisher. This saves 3-4 handgun rounds per enemy. On a full playthrough, this technique conserves roughly 150-200 handgun rounds — enough to carry you through ammo-scarce sections.

TIP 2

Match Your Weapon to the Enemy Type

Using the wrong weapon wastes ammo. Handgun vs. Crimson Lycan: 20+ rounds. Shotgun vs. Crimson Lycan: 4 shells. Rifle vs. Security Drone: 2-3 shots. Shotgun vs. Security Drone: 6+ shells (waste). Always switch to the optimal weapon for each enemy — the weapon wheel slowdown gives you time to assess.

TIP 3

Use Explosive Canisters for Crowd Control

Red canisters are found throughout the game and deal massive AoE damage. One canister explosion kills all basic infected in range and deals roughly 30% of a Crimson Lycan's HP. In arena encounters like the Chapter X convergence fights, canisters respawn every 30 seconds — use them as your primary damage source.

TIP 4

Enemy Priority in Mixed Encounters

When facing multiple enemy types, the kill order is: Shadow Wraiths > Crimson Lycans > Phantom Hounds > Ranged Cultists > Everything Else. Shadow Wraiths and Crimson Lycans are the only enemies that can kill you in one combo on higher difficulties. Clear them first, then mop up.

TIP 5

Sound Design Is Gameplay Information

RE Requiem's audio design is not just atmospheric. Phantom Hounds emit a distinct howl before dashing. Crimson Lycans growl before leaping. Shadow Wraiths produce a low-frequency hum when invisible and nearby. Play with headphones and learn these audio cues — they give you a 1-2 second warning before every major attack.
