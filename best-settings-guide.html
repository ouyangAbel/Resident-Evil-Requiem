SHADOW QUALITY — THE BIGGEST PERFORMANCE HOG

If you change only one setting to improve performance, make it Shadow Quality. The jump from High to Max shadows costs approximately 18–22% of your frame rate on mid-range GPUs, while the visual difference is nearly imperceptible outside of side-by-side screenshot comparisons. Setting shadows to Medium saves roughly 12% FPS over High with only a slight reduction in shadow draw distance — well worth the tradeoff for action-heavy encounters. For budget systems, Low shadows are entirely playable and yield a massive 28% FPS gain over Max.

VOLUMETRIC LIGHTING — ATMOSPHERE AT A COST

Volumetric lighting is responsible for the thick fog, god rays, and atmospheric haze that define RE Requiem's oppressive visual identity. It is also the second-most expensive setting after shadows. High volumetric lighting costs roughly 15% FPS over Low on mid-range GPUs. Our recommendation: Medium preserves the moody atmosphere while recovering about 8% performance over High. The difference between Medium and High is mostly visible in the Dimensional Rift sequences — if you are struggling for frames, drop to Low and the game still looks excellent in 90% of scenes.

RAY TRACING — BEAUTIFUL BUT BRUTAL

RE Requiem supports ray-traced reflections and ray-traced shadows. RT reflections are stunning in the laboratory's glass corridors and the Rookwood Estate's polished marble floors, but enabling them costs approximately 35–40% of your frame rate on an RTX 4070 at 1440p. RT shadows are far less impactful visually — the standard shadow maps are already excellent — and cost an additional 12–15% FPS. Unless you are running an RTX 4080 or 4090 at 1440p (or using DLSS Performance mode to compensate), we recommend leaving both RT options off for the smoothest gameplay experience. In a game where frame timing matters for Perfect Dodge execution, stable high FPS beats shinier puddles every time.

PRO TIP

Use Variable Rate Shading (VRS)

RE Requiem's hidden VRS setting (accessible via the config.ini file in Documents\Capcom\RE_Requiem\) can yield an additional 5–8% FPS with almost zero visual impact. Set VRS=1 in the [Render] section. VRS intelligently reduces shading quality in peripheral areas of the screen where you will not notice the difference, while keeping the crosshair center at full fidelity — a perfect fit for a survival horror game where you are constantly scanning the environment.

DISPLAY & RESOLUTION SETTINGS

HDR CALIBRATION

RE Requiem's HDR implementation is one of the best in the RE Engine library. If you have an HDR-capable display (HDR600 or better), enable HDR and use these calibration values for the most atmospheric horror experience:

  • Peak Brightness: Set to your display's actual peak nits (typically 600–1000). If unsure, start at 800 and adjust until the white calibration box is barely visible against the background.
  • Paper White: 250 nits — this controls UI and menu brightness independently from the game world.
  • Shadow Detail: -2 to -4 — RE Requiem is a horror game. Crushing the blacks slightly enhances the oppressive atmosphere and hides visual artifacts in the darkest areas of the Rookwood Estate cellar and the Dimensional Rift.
  • Color Saturation: Default (0). The game's color grading is deliberately desaturated; boosting saturation ruins the intended aesthetic.

FIELD OF VIEW (FOV)

The default FOV in RE Requiem is quite narrow — approximately 75 degrees horizontal — which contributes to the claustrophobic horror feel but can cause motion sickness for some players. The FOV slider ranges from 65 to 100. Our recommendation:

  • Immersive Horror: 75–80 (default range, maximum tension)
  • Comfortable Play: 85–90 (good balance of immersion and spatial awareness)
  • Competitive / Speedrun: 95–100 (maximum peripheral awareness for enemy detection)

ULTRAWIDE SUPPORT: RE Requiem supports 21:9 and 32:9 ultrawide resolutions natively, but cutscenes are rendered in 16:9 with pillar boxes. To remove the pillar boxes, add AspectRatioFix=1 to the config.ini file. Note that this may reveal off-screen geometry or characters during some pre-rendered sequences — a minor immersion break, but well worth it for uninterrupted ultrawide gameplay.

WHY R1 DODGE?

The Perfect Dodge Binding Advantage

Moving Dodge/Crouch from Circle/B to R1/RB is the single most impactful controller remap in RE Requiem. On the default layout, you must remove your thumb from the right stick (camera control) to dodge, which means you cannot aim and dodge simultaneously. With R1 dodge, your index finger handles dodging while your thumb stays on the right stick for continuous camera control. This makes Perfect Dodges dramatically more consistent, especially against Shadow Stalker and the Forgotten King, where split-second directional awareness is critical. MOUSE PRO TIP

Disable Mouse Smoothing in Config

RE Requiem applies mouse smoothing by default, which introduces input lag. To disable it, navigate to Documents\Capcom\RE_Requiem\config.ini and set MouseSmoothing=0 and MouseAcceleration=0 in the [Input] section. This alone can reduce perceived input latency by 12–18ms, making flick shots and reactive Perfect Dodges significantly more responsive.

FAQ: RESIDENT EVIL REQUIEM BEST SETTINGS

Q1

What are the absolute best settings for Resident Evil Requiem on PC?

For most players, the optimal Resident Evil Requiem best settings are: Texture Quality High, Shadows Medium, Volumetric Lighting Medium, Ambient Occlusion HBAO+, Screen Space Reflections ON, DLSS Quality (NVIDIA) or FSR Quality (AMD), Motion Blur OFF, Film Grain OFF, Frame Rate Cap 120 FPS. This configuration delivers excellent visual quality while maintaining high and stable frame rates on any mid-range or better GPU. For specific hardware recommendations, see the full table in the Graphics Settings section above.

Q2

How do I fix stuttering in Resident Evil Requiem?

RE Requiem stuttering is almost always caused by VRAM exhaustion. If your GPU has 8 GB or less VRAM, reduce Texture Quality to Medium (1 GB) or Low, and disable Ray Tracing entirely. Additionally, ensure your GPU drivers are updated to the latest Game Ready version (NVIDIA 560.xx+ or AMD Adrenalin 24.7.1+), as both include RE Requiem-specific optimizations. Finally, add -NOTEXTURESTREAMING to your Steam launch options to disable background texture streaming, which is the primary cause of traversal stutter during dimensional rift transitions.

Q3

Should I use DLSS or FSR in RE Requiem?

If you have an NVIDIA RTX card (20-series or newer), use DLSS Quality — it provides better image stability, fewer artifacts in motion, and superior anti-aliasing compared to FSR. For AMD or older NVIDIA cards, FSR Quality is a solid alternative, though you may notice slight ghosting on fast-moving enemies. Avoid DLSS/FSR Performance or Ultra Performance modes below 4K resolution, as the internal render resolution drops enough to visibly soften textures and introduce shimmer on fine details like chain-link fences and character hair.

Q4

What is the best controller layout for Perfect Dodge?

The single best controller remap for RE Requiem is moving Dodge/Crouch from Circle/B to R1/RB. This allows you to dodge without removing your thumb from the right analog stick, maintaining continuous camera control during evasive maneuvers. Combined with the Type C control scheme (R2/RT to fire) and practiced timing from our Perfect Dodge Guide, this binding makes consistent Perfect Dodges achievable even on Professional difficulty.

Q5

Does RE Requiem support ultrawide monitors?

Yes, RE Requiem fully supports 21:9 (2560×1080, 3440×1440) and 32:9 (5120×1440) ultrawide resolutions natively during gameplay. However, cutscenes are letterboxed to 16:9 by default. To force ultrawide cutscenes, add AspectRatioFix=1 to the [Display] section of config.ini. The in-game HUD and UI scale correctly to ultrawide aspect ratios, and the expanded horizontal field of view provides a genuine gameplay advantage by letting you spot enemies approaching from the sides earlier.

Q6

How much VRAM does Resident Evil Requiem actually need?

At 1080p with Medium textures: 4–5 GB. At 1440p with High textures: 7–8 GB. At 4K with High textures and RT reflections: 11–12 GB. The game's VRAM estimator in the graphics menu is accurate to within ±0.5 GB. If your GPU has 8 GB VRAM, you can run 1440p High textures comfortably but should avoid the 3 GB texture pool setting and RT reflections. Running out of VRAM causes severe hitching and texture pop-in — if you experience these symptoms, drop your texture quality by one tier immediately.

FINAL THOUGHTS: THE PERFECT BALANCE

Resident Evil Requiem is a technical showcase that rewards thoughtful settings configuration. The difference between the default "Recommended" preset and a manually optimized configuration can be 30% more FPS with zero perceptible visual downgrade. The RE Engine scales beautifully across hardware tiers — the game looks atmospheric and menacing even on Low settings at 1080p, and absolutely jaw-dropping on Ultra with RT at 4K.

The key takeaway: Shadows and Volumetric Lighting are your two biggest performance levers. Drop these before touching anything else. Motion Blur and Film Grain should be off for any serious playthrough. And if you have an RTX card, DLSS Quality is essentially free performance — enable it and forget it. Most importantly, a stable frame rate beats a high average frame rate every time. Cap your FPS at a level your system can hold consistently, and you will have a smoother, more responsive survival horror experience than someone chasing uncapped frames with constant dips.
