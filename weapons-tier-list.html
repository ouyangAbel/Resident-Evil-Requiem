How we ranked each weapon: DPS is calculated as (damage per shot × fire rate × accuracy multiplier) / reload time. Ratings assume fully upgraded stats. S-TIER: THE BEST OF THE BEST

S — DOMINANT

Semi-Auto Rifle — The undisputed king of RE Requiem weapons. With a fully upgraded DPS of 342, it outguns every weapon in the game by a significant margin. The combination of high per-shot damage, fast fire rate, and excellent ammo efficiency makes it the clear first upgrade priority.

  • Damage per shot: 48 (upgraded)
  • Fire rate: 7.1 rounds/sec
  • Accuracy: 94%
  • Ammo efficiency: 1 kill per 2-3 shots on Standard
  • Best for: Every combat scenario

Location: Found in Chapter IV — Estate Cellar, behind the locked door requiring the Cellar Key. Available from mid-game onward.

Upgrade path: Prioritize Damage Level 3 first (unlocks at 12,000 Pesetas), then Fire Rate Level 2. The Damage 3 upgrade alone pushes DPS from 210 to 342 — a 63% increase that pays for itself within two chapters.

Pro Tip: The Semi-Auto Rifle's scope has a hidden zoom function. Tap the aim button twice while scoped for 2x magnification — invaluable for sniping Lycan weakpoints from safety. Combine with perfect dodge for maximum DPS uptime.

A-TIER: STRONG PICKS

A — EXCELLENT

These weapons are outstanding performers that narrowly miss S-tier due to ammo constraints or situational utility. Both deserve upgrade investment if you have the resources.

Broken Butterfly Magnum (A-Tier — 248 DPS)

The most powerful handgun in the game, the Broken Butterfly delivers devastating single-shot damage. Its DPS ranks second only to the Semi-Auto Rifle. However, magnum ammo is extremely scarce — you'll find fewer than 60 rounds in a full playthrough. This weapon is strictly for boss encounters.

  • Damage per shot: 380 (upgraded)
  • Fire rate: 0.65 rounds/sec
  • Effective DPS on bosses: 248 (highest stagger)
  • Ammo found: ~58 rounds total
  • Location: Defeat The Forgotten King (optional boss, Chapter IX) or purchase from Merchant in Chapter X

Striker Shotgun (A-Tier — 235 DPS)

The Striker is the superior shotgun variant, offering tighter pellet spread and faster reload than the W-870. Its DPS peaks when firing at point-blank range against clustered enemies — perfect for Chapter VI's laboratory corridors. The 12-shell drum magazine provides sustained fire that the W-870 simply cannot match.

B-TIER: SOLID BUT SITUATIONAL

B — RELIABLE

B-tier weapons perform well in their intended roles but lack the dominance of higher tiers. They're worth upgrading if you prefer their playstyle, but shouldn't consume your first upgrade slots.

M19 Handgun (B-Tier — 198 DPS)

The starting handgun is surprisingly competitive. After full upgrades, its DPS nearly doubles from the base 105. While it won't replace your primary weapon, it serves as an excellent backup for finishing off wounded enemies without wasting rifle ammo.

CQBR Assault Rifle (B-Tier — 189 DPS)

Found in Chapter VIII, the CQBR trades single-shot damage for volume of fire. Its 30-round magazine and full-auto mode shred weaker enemies but struggles against armored targets. Best used as a crowd-clearing tool in the Forgotten City segments.

Riot Shotgun (B-Tier — 210 DPS)

The Riot Shotgun sits between the W-870 and Striker in performance. It has a faster pump-action cycle than the W-870 but less range than the Striker. A solid choice if you missed the Striker or prefer mid-range shotgun engagements.

C-TIER: UNDERWHELMING

C — UNDERPOWERED

These weapons function but consistently underperform compared to their alternatives. Only upgrade these if you're going for 100% completion or playing on lower difficulties.

W-870 Shotgun (C-Tier — 175 DPS)

The first shotgun you find is also the weakest. Slow reload, wide pellet spread, and limited range make it inferior to both the Striker and Riot Shotgun in every metric. Use it until you find better options, then shelf it.

LE 5 SMG (C-Tier — 165 DPS)

The SMG's low per-shot damage means you burn through 9mm ammo at an alarming rate. Even fully upgraded, it requires an entire magazine to down a single Hardcore Lycan. The only redeeming quality is its stagger value against humanoid enemies.

Combat Knife (C-Tier — 120 DPS)

Despite being a fan-favorite, the knife's base DPS is negligible against late-game enemies. However, master the parry counter (perfect dodge + knife slash) to deal 400+ damage in a single hit. For advanced players only — beginners should rely on firearms.

D-TIER: SKIP THESE

D — AVOID

D-tier weapons consume inventory slots and ammo types you need for better options. Never invest upgrade materials in these — the returns simply aren't worth it.

M1911 Handgun (D-Tier — 142 DPS)

The M1911 looks cool but performs worse than the starting M19 in every category. Its slower fire rate and identical ammo type make it a strict downgrade. Find it in Chapter VII and leave it in storage.

SLS 60 (D-Tier — 138 DPS)

A snub-nosed revolver with pitiful range and damage. The SLS 60 exists to be replaced by the Broken Butterfly as quickly as possible. Never upgrade — the materials are better spent elsewhere.

Grenade Launcher (D-Tier — 95 DPS)

The Grenade Launcher looks powerful but its DPS is crippled by a 3-second reload and rare ammo supply. Its real value is crowd control against swarms, not damage. Pick it up for Chapter XII's mass enemy waves, then drop it.

UPGRADE PRIORITY GUIDE

With limited Pesetas and upgrade materials, you can only fully upgrade 3-4 weapons per playthrough. Follow this priority list to maximize your combat effectiveness:

  1. Semi-Auto Rifle Damage 3 — 12,000 Pesetas. This single upgrade provides the highest ROI in the game, boosting DPS by 63%.
  2. Broken Butterfly Damage 2 — 8,500 Pesetas. Enough to one-shot most enemies on Standard and stagger bosses reliably.
  3. Striker Shotgun Reload Speed 2 — 6,200 Pesetas. The reload upgrade is more valuable than damage for shotguns — every second counts in close combat.
  4. M19 Handgun Fire Rate 2 — 4,000 Pesetas. Cheap and effective, turns the starter handgun into a reliable sidearm.

Budget Build for Hardcore: If you're struggling with ammo, skip the Broken Butterfly upgrades and invest fully in the Semi-Auto Rifle + M19 Handgun combo. The Rifle handles 90% of encounters, and the M19 covers the rest with minimal resource drain.

DIFFICULTY-SPECIFIC RECOMMENDATIONS

The best weapons shift depending on which difficulty you play. Here's how the tier list changes across Standard, Hardcore, and Professional:

Standard Difficulty

With generous ammo drops, the Semi-Auto Rifle dominates every encounter. The Broken Butterfly is overkill — save those magnum rounds for Hardcore. Upgrade priority: Rifle → M19 → Striker (for fun).

Hardcore Difficulty

Ammo scarcity makes efficiency king. The M19 Handgun fully upgraded becomes a legitimate primary weapon — its 9mm ammo is abundant and it kills Lycans in 4-5 shots. Pair with the Rifle for armored enemies. The Broken Butterfly is essential for boss fights but conserve every round.

Professional Difficulty

On Professional, enemy health is doubled and ammo halved. The tier list inverts slightly: the Broken Butterfly becomes S-tier because single-shot kills are mandatory. The Rifle drops to A-tier because enemies survive longer, requiring more rounds per kill. Perfect dodge becomes your primary survival tool — read our perfect dodge guide to master it.

Professional Tip: On Professional difficulty, never fire more than 3 shots at a single enemy. If they're still standing, dodge and reposition. Wasted ammo is the #1 cause of failure on Professional.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What is the best weapon in Resident Evil Requiem?

The Semi-Auto Rifle is the best overall weapon. Its fully upgraded DPS of 342 is 38% higher than the next-best option, and its ammo efficiency means you can use it as a primary weapon throughout the entire game without running dry.

Should I upgrade the Shotgun or the Rifle first?

Always upgrade the Rifle first. The Shotgun's DPS falls off sharply beyond point-blank range, while the Rifle excels at all distances. The Rifle's Damage 3 upgrade alone costs 12,000 Pesetas and provides a massive 63% DPS increase — better than any shotgun upgrade.

Is the Broken Butterfly Magnum worth the investment?

For boss fights — absolutely. The Broken Butterfly's 380 damage per shot is unmatched and can skip boss phases entirely. However, magnum ammo is scarce (only ~58 rounds in a full game), so never use it on regular enemies. Upgrade Damage 2 for bosses, then stop.

Which weapon should I use in the Mercenaries mode?

In Mercenaries, the Striker Shotgun is king. The scoring system rewards melee finishers and rapid kills, and the Striker's 12-shell drum lets you chain combos without reloading. Pair with the Combat Knife for melee bonus points.

Does the Knife have any practical use?

The Knife ranks C-tier for general combat but becomes viable when you master the parry counter. Perfect dodge + knife slash deals 400+ damage — enough to one-shot most enemies. It's a high-risk, high-reward tool for experienced players. Beginners should avoid relying on it.
