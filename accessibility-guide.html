RESIDENT EVIL REQUIEM ACCESSIBILITY GUIDE: SETTINGS, SUBTITLES & DIFFICULTY OPTIONS FOR EVERY PLAYER

UPDATED JULY 9, 2026 • 16 MIN READ
SHARE THIS

Resident Evil Requiem is Capcom's most ambitious survival horror game to date, and accessibility has become an increasingly important part of the conversation around modern game design. Whether you have limited mobility, hearing difficulties, visual impairments, or simply prefer a more customized experience, Resident Evil Requiem offers a surprisingly robust set of accessibility settings that can dramatically improve your playthrough. This comprehensive Resident Evil Requiem accessibility guide covers every option in the game — from subtitle customization and colorblind modes to controller remapping, difficulty adjustments, and audio cue settings — so every player can experience Requiem's terrifying world on their own terms.

Accessibility Note: All accessibility settings in Resident Evil Requiem can be changed at any time from the pause menu — you do not need to restart the game or create a new save. Experiment freely to find what works best for you.

SUBTITLE & CAPTION SETTINGS

Subtitles are one of the most commonly requested accessibility features, and Resident Evil Requiem delivers a comprehensive subtitle system that goes beyond simple text display. The game offers three subtitle modes, adjustable font sizing, background opacity controls, and speaker identification — making it one of the most customizable subtitle systems in the Resident Evil franchise.

Subtitle Modes

Navigate to Options → Display → Subtitles to access the following settings:

Pro Tip: Set subtitles to "Full" with "Speaker Name: On" during your first playthrough. Resident Evil Requiem has several scenes where off-screen characters speak — knowing who is talking and from which direction can be the difference between life and death in scripted ambush sequences.

Closed Captions for Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing Players

The Closed Captions option is distinct from subtitles and specifically designed for deaf and hard-of-hearing players. When enabled, the game displays bracketed descriptions of environmental and gameplay sounds — things like [BEHIND: HEAVY BREATHING], [ABOVE: CEILING CREAKING], and [RIGHT: DISTANT EXPLOSION]. These directional cues are especially valuable during stealth sections and boss encounters where audio positioning is critical for survival.

Unlike many games that offer generic sound descriptions, RE Requiem's closed captions include directional indicators (LEFT, RIGHT, BEHIND, ABOVE, BELOW) and distance qualifiers (CLOSE, DISTANT) that convey spatial information usually delivered through surround sound. This makes the game fully playable without audio for the first time in the series.

VISUAL ACCESSIBILITY OPTIONS

Resident Evil Requiem includes several visual accessibility features that address colorblindness, low vision, and photosensitivity — areas where the survival horror genre has historically been weak.

Colorblind Modes

Navigate to Options → Display → Colorblind Assist to find three dedicated filters:

MODETYPEWHAT IT CHANGES
ProtanopiaRed-weakShifts red hues toward orange/yellow; affects enemy health bars, item highlights, and warning indicators
DeuteranopiaGreen-weakShifts green hues toward blue/amber; affects herb colors, safe indicators, and map markers
TritanopiaBlue-weakShifts blue hues toward green/red; affects UI accents, item rarity borders, and waypoint colors

Each colorblind mode adjusts the game's entire color pipeline — not just UI elements. This means environmental cues (like the red glow of a save room typewriter or the green shimmer of a healing herb) are also corrected. The filter intensity can be adjusted from 0% to 100% with a real-time preview slider, so you can fine-tune the correction to match your specific vision profile.

Herb Color Accessibility: The classic Resident Evil herb system (green = heal, red = boost, yellow = cure) has historically been problematic for colorblind players. In RE Requiem, enabling any colorblind mode also adds distinct shape indicators to herb icons — a cross for green, a diamond for red, and a circle for yellow — so you can identify herbs by shape alone.

Screen Shake & Photosensitivity

Resident Evil Requiem contains significant screen shake during explosions, boss encounters, and scripted events. For players sensitive to motion effects, the following settings are available under Options → Display → Visual Effects:

  • Screen Shake Intensity: Adjustable from 0% (off) to 100% (default). Setting to 0% removes all camera wobble while preserving gameplay impact.
  • Chromatic Aberration: Off / Low / High. This post-processing effect can cause eye strain and headaches for some players.
  • Motion Blur: Off / Low / Medium / High. Disabling motion blur improves clarity during fast camera movements.
  • Flashing Lights Reduction: On / Off. Replaces strobing and rapid flash effects with a steady glow. Essential for photosensitive players.
  • Film Grain: Off / Low / High. Removes the noise overlay that simulates analog film texture.

Photosensitivity Warning: Chapter 6 ("The Living Laboratory") and Chapter 10 ("Convergence") contain intense strobe sequences. If you are photosensitive, enable "Flashing Lights Reduction" before reaching these chapters. The setting replaces strobe effects with a sustained soft glow that conveys the same narrative intensity without the risk.

HUD & Text Scaling

Under Options → Display → HUD, you can adjust the size and opacity of all heads-up display elements:

  • HUD Scale: 80% / 90% / 100% / 110% / 120% / 150%. Scales health bar, ammo counter, minimap, and item notifications.
  • HUD Opacity: 50% to 100% in 10% increments.
  • Minimap Always On: Off / On. When enabled, the minimap remains visible even during combat (normally hides during encounters).
  • Objective Marker: Off / On / Persistent. "Persistent" keeps the objective marker on-screen at all times with a directional arrow, even when the target is behind you.

CONTROLLER & INPUT REMAPPING

Resident Evil Requiem supports full button remapping for both controllers and keyboard/mouse — a critical accessibility feature for players with limited mobility or those using adaptive controllers.

Hold vs. Toggle Options

Many actions in Resident Evil Requiem default to "hold" inputs (hold to aim, hold to run, hold to sprint). For players with grip strength limitations or those using adaptive controllers, each of these can be switched to "toggle" mode:

  • Aim Mode: Hold / Toggle. Toggle means press LT once to enter aiming, press again to lower the weapon.
  • Sprint Mode: Hold / Toggle. Toggle means press once to start running, press again to stop.
  • Block / Guard: Hold / Toggle. Available for melee-focused playstyles.
  • Continuous Interaction: Off / On. When enabled, holding the interact button automatically completes all hold-to-interact prompts (doors, levers, puzzle items) without repeated input.

Adaptive Controller Support: RE Requiem has native support for the Xbox Adaptive Controller and PlayStation Access Controller. All remapping works with these devices, and the game recognizes them as standard controllers with no additional configuration needed.

Keyboard & Mouse Remapping

PC players get full keyboard remapping under Options → Controls → Key Bindings. Every action can be bound to any key, and the game supports modifier keys (Shift+, Ctrl+, Alt+) for expanded binding options. Mouse buttons 4 and 5 (side buttons) are fully supported and can be bound to any action. Additionally, mouse sensitivity can be adjusted separately for normal camera movement (1–100) and aiming (1–100), with an independent ADS (aim-down-sights) multiplier.

DIFFICULTY & GAMEPLAY ADJUSTMENTS

Resident Evil Requiem's difficulty system has significant accessibility implications. The game offers four difficulty levels, and unlike some entries in the series, you can switch difficulty at any time from the pause menu without losing progress.

Assisted Mode Accessibility Features

Assisted difficulty is more than just reduced numbers — it includes several built-in accessibility enhancements:

  • Auto-Aim Assist: When aiming near an enemy, the crosshair gently snaps toward the nearest vulnerable point. Sensitivity is adjustable from Low to High.
  • Auto-Recovery: When health drops to "Danger" (red), it slowly regenerates to "Caution" (yellow) over 30 seconds. This eliminates the pressure of finding healing items during tense sequences.
  • Quick Time Event (QTE) Assist: QTEs automatically succeed after a 3-second grace period. You can still complete them manually for faster results, but failure is impossible.
  • Reduced Enemy Aggression: Enemies attack less frequently, have longer telegraph windows, and are less likely to ambush from behind.
  • Unlimited Ink Ribbons: Save at any typewriter without consuming items (Standard and above require ink ribbons on Professional).

No Difficulty Trophies: Resident Evil Requiem does NOT have difficulty-specific trophies or achievements. You can play the entire game on Assisted and still earn the Platinum trophy. This is a deliberate accessibility decision by Capcom — play at whatever difficulty brings you the most enjoyment.

AUDIO ACCESSIBILITY SETTINGS

Audio is central to the survival horror experience, and RE Requiem provides several options to customize the audio experience for different needs.

Audio Mix Profiles

Under Options → Audio → Mix Profile, you can select from several pre-configured audio mixes:

PROFILEDESCRIPTION
DefaultStandard horror mix — emphasizes ambient tension, whisper-quiet enemies, dramatic music swells
HeadphonesOptimized for stereo headphones — wider spatial separation, enhanced positional audio
TV SpeakersCompressed dynamic range — dialogue is louder relative to effects, bass is reduced
Night ModeHeavy dynamic range compression — quiet sounds are louder, loud sounds are quieter. Ideal for playing without disturbing others.
AccessibilityMaximum dialogue clarity, reduced ambient noise, enhanced directional cues. Best for hard-of-hearing players.

Individual Volume Sliders

Beyond the mix profiles, RE Requiem offers granular volume control for five separate channels:

  • Master Volume: 0–100
  • Music: 0–100 — controls the dynamic score that intensifies during combat
  • Sound Effects: 0–100 — footsteps, gunshots, environmental sounds
  • Dialogue: 0–100 — spoken lines and radio communications
  • Ambient: 0–100 — background atmosphere, wind, creaking, distant sounds

For hard-of-hearing players, the recommended setup is: Dialogue at 100, Music at 40, Ambient at 30, and Sound Effects at 80. This prioritizes spoken content while reducing the atmospheric layer that can mask important audio cues.

3D Audio & Spatial Sound

On PS5, Resident Evil Requiem supports Tempest 3D Audio for headphones, providing accurate vertical and horizontal positioning of enemy sounds. On PC, the game supports both Windows Sonic and Dolby Atmos for Headphones. These spatial audio technologies are particularly valuable for locating enemies in dark environments where visual confirmation is limited — an accessibility benefit for players who rely more heavily on audio cues.

GAMEPLAY ASSIST OPTIONS

Beyond difficulty settings, Resident Evil Requiem includes several standalone gameplay assists that can be toggled independently of difficulty level:

  • Aim Snap Strength: Off / Low / Medium / High. Adjusts how strongly the crosshair pulls toward enemies when you aim near them.
  • Aim Slowdown: Off / On. Reduces camera speed when the crosshair passes over an enemy, making it easier to stay on target.
  • Auto-Item Pickup: Off / On. Items are automatically collected when you walk near them, eliminating the need to press an interact button.
  • Puzzle Hint System: Off / Delayed / Immediate. "Delayed" shows a hint after 2 minutes of no progress; "Immediate" shows the solution approach right away.
  • Navigation Assist: Off / On. Adds a persistent breadcrumb trail on the ground leading to your current objective. Colors change based on distance (green = close, yellow = medium, red = far).
  • QTE Auto-Complete: Off / On (Independent of difficulty). Automatically completes all quick-time events regardless of difficulty setting.

Key Point: All gameplay assists work on ANY difficulty, including Hardcore and Professional. You can play on Professional with auto-aim and navigation assist enabled. There is no penalty for using assists — trophies, unlocks, and rankings are not affected.

PLATFORM-SPECIFIC ACCESSIBILITY NOTES

PS5

The PS5 version supports the PlayStation Access Controller natively. Additionally, the DualSense controller's haptic feedback can be adjusted independently: Vibration Intensity (Off / Weak / Medium / Strong) and Adaptive Triggers (Off / On). Turning off adaptive triggers reduces the resistance on L2/R2, which can be helpful for players with grip strength limitations. The PS5's built-in system-level accessibility features (text-to-speech, button remapping, high contrast) also work with RE Requiem.

Xbox Series X|S

The Xbox version has full compatibility with the Xbox Adaptive Controller. The Xbox system-level Copilot feature — which lets two controllers act as one — also works with RE Requiem, allowing a second player to assist with inputs if needed.

PC

PC players benefit from additional customization through config file editing. The file re_quiem_config.ini (located in %USERPROFILE%\Documents\My Games\RE_Requiem\) exposes additional settings not available in the in-game menu, including custom field-of-view values, frame rate cap adjustments, and raw input sensitivity multipliers. For detailed PC optimization, see our Best Settings Guide.

FINAL THOUGHTS ON RE REQUIEM ACCESSIBILITY

Resident Evil Requiem represents a significant step forward for accessibility in the survival horror genre. Capcom has clearly studied what studios like Naughty Dog (The Last of Us Part II) and Santa Monica Studio (God of War Ragnarök) have accomplished with accessibility, and brought many of those principles into the Resident Evil framework without compromising the series' signature tension and challenge.

The key insight is that RE Requiem's accessibility options are granular and independent — you can enable auto-aim without touching difficulty, activate closed captions without enabling subtitles, or reduce screen shake without disabling any visual effects. This modular approach means you can create a perfectly tailored experience rather than choosing between "accessible" and "default." Every setting can be changed at any time, and no accessibility option disables trophies, unlocks, or ranking.
